## Decisions Pam made this session (all recorded in the docs)

- Fees: protocol 0.5% of seller payout plus 0.5% of logistics payout, never added to the buyer total and never higher. Marketplace fee 0 to 5%, default 2% (free months is a proposal only). Marketplace fee must be collected at payout and shown live.
- Server-held wallet accepted for v1.
- Disputes: B2B model with closed grounds, window 7 / 14 / 30 days, marketplace arbitrates and the protocol pays out automatically, 5-day seller response, grace 3 days with a buyer-approved extension capped at 30 days in total, 24-hour hold only on **large** (USD 5,000 or more) or **overridden** rulings. **Anonymous parties: automatic rules only.** Long-term aim: 99% automatic and learning, rule changes through governance.
- Logistics pool: KYC only. Nostr identities may use it once KYC is done.
- Quotes are valid until their date, withdrawn early only for global shocks (then they become estimates). Multi-vendor orders ship separately unless the buyer arranges consolidation.
- The protocol should hold no KYC personal data and no password hashes (D58). Session 28 designed it so; the code kept them in the protocol as a v1 shortcut.
- File names in `docs/` are lowercase.
- Protocol freezes: five justifications only. Court orders are not one.
- Council: 2 of 3, no named signers. **Pam holds the emergency pause for now.**
- Identity: keep KYC, Nostr and anonymous. Logistics is KYC only, anonymous users arrange their own.
- Search: Postgres full-text with ranking for launch.
- Nostr: build it like Buzz with a likely centralised relay, run by the protocol for now. Marketplaces may later run their own small relays. Lightning is on the to-do list (needs a node).
- Added 20 Nov: grace extensions capped at 30 days in total, USD 5,000 is the "large" threshold, anonymous parties are decided by automatic rules only.
- Added 20 Nov, marketplaces: public track record only, no bond. Anonymous and Nostr identities may open marketplaces. The first KYC checks of marketplace owners are done by hand by the people running the protocol, until about the fifth marketplace.
- Added 20 Nov: the protocol holds no legal names, registration numbers, tax IDs, addresses or ID documents for anyone, but keeps non-identifying metadata for its own improvement. The password and KYC storage in the protocol database is a shortcut that must be fixed before v1 (D58).
- Added 20 Nov, returns: confirm it is a real problem, then whose fault. The party at fault pays: carrier for shipment faults, seller for seller faults.
